# Grant Operations

Run grants as an operating system, not a loose collection of deadlines.

Use the lifecycle:

`discover -> intake -> qualify -> decide -> plan -> collect -> draft -> review -> submit -> award setup -> deliver -> monitor -> report -> amend if needed -> renew/close`

The objective is to make grant work visible, owned, evidence-backed, reviewable, and ready for human decision at the right points.

## Core operating rules

1. Treat funder guidelines, notices, award documents, approved organizational records, approved program facts, approved budgets, and connected systems as sources of truth.
2. Separate facts, assumptions, open questions, recommendations, and decisions.
3. Never infer eligibility, deadlines, time zones, match requirements, allowable costs, certifications, indirect cost treatment, reporting obligations, award terms, or renewal conditions when the source is unclear.
4. Never invent program outcomes, demographic claims, financial figures, organizational credentials, partnerships, or prior performance.
5. Mark stale, conflicting, incomplete, or unsupported information explicitly.
6. Preserve human approval for consequential actions.
7. Minimize sensitive data. Do not request or repeat bank credentials, passwords, unnecessary donor data, unnecessary beneficiary data, or other information the task does not require.
8. Prefer structured operating artifacts over long prose when a checklist, register, matrix, calendar, brief, or dashboard will help the team act.

## Start by identifying the operating context

Before doing substantial work, determine what is available and what is missing:

- organization and program
- funder and opportunity
- funding type if known
- source documents
- application or award stage
- internal owner
- collaborators and reviewers
- submission or reporting channel
- deadline and time zone if explicitly sourced
- approved budget source
- approval authority
- current status
- unresolved questions

Do not block useful work when information is missing. Produce the best supported artifact and place unknowns in an `Open questions` or `Needs verification` section.

# 1. Grant pipeline and opportunity intake

When the user is considering multiple opportunities, create a pipeline rather than treating each grant in isolation.

Track, when available:

- opportunity / program name
- funder
- source URL or source document
- stage
- deadline / time zone
- award range
- stated period of performance
- stated eligibility
- geography or population restrictions
- stated match / cost share
- purpose / program fit
- likely internal owner
- estimated internal effort
- required partners or attachments
- open eligibility questions
- go / hold / no-go decision status
- next action
- next review date

Recommended stages:

`watching | triage | qualifying | approved to pursue | application in progress | internal review | ready to submit | submitted | pending decision | awarded | declined | withdrawn | closed`

Do not turn missing information into a negative score. Missing information is a verification task.

# 2. Qualification and go/no-go decision support

For a single opportunity, produce a `Grant Qualification Brief`.

Assess only against sourced evidence:

- eligibility
- mission and program fit
- population / geography fit
- use-of-funds fit
- funding amount and duration
- match / cost-share burden
- staffing and delivery capacity
- data / evaluation expectations
- reporting burden
- partnership requirements
- financial / cash-flow considerations
- restrictions or unusual terms
- strategic value
- internal effort and timing

Output:

## Grant Qualification Brief

- Opportunity
- Funder
- Deadline
- What the funder is trying to fund
- Confirmed eligibility facts
- Fit strengths
- Risks / burdens
- Missing information
- Required internal capabilities
- Decision needed
- Recommendation: `pursue | hold pending verification | do not pursue`
- Recommendation rationale
- Human decision owner

A recommendation is advisory only. The human owner makes the go/no-go decision.

# 3. Application operating plan

Once an opportunity is approved to pursue, create a backward plan from the sourced submission deadline.

Build:

- major milestones
- internal draft dates
- budget completion date
- evidence / attachment due dates
- partner input due dates
- review rounds
- executive / authorized signatory review
- portal preparation
- final quality control
- designated submitter
- submission target earlier than the external deadline when practical

For each task show:

`deliverable | owner | source/input | due date | dependency | status | reviewer | approval required | risk`

Do not invent dates when the external deadline is unknown. Use relative sequencing until a sourced deadline is available.

# 4. Requirements and evidence matrix

Translate a funding notice or application package into a controlled requirements matrix.

Use columns:

`requirement | source citation/location | interpretation | evidence needed | owner | status | gap | due date | reviewer | approval gate`

Capture:

- eligibility documentation
- registration requirements
- narrative sections
- budgets and budget narratives
- attachments
- letters / MOUs
- organizational documents
- data / evaluation requirements
- certifications and representations
- signatures
- portal fields
- formatting / page limits
- submission rules

Distinguish:

- `confirmed requirement`
- `appears applicable — verify`
- `not found in source`

# 5. Organizational evidence and reusable source bank

Help the team build a reusable source bank from approved material instead of rewriting basic facts every cycle.

Possible categories:

- mission and organizational description
- program descriptions
- service geography
- populations served
- approved outcome definitions
- approved historical results
- leadership and governance information
- staff bios
- audited financial references
- approved budgets
- IRS / registration information
- partner information
- standard attachments
- evaluation methods
- data collection methods

For each reusable item track:

`artifact | approved source | owner | last verified | approved uses | restrictions | refresh date`

Never convert an old proposal claim into a current fact without verification.

# 6. Narrative development

Draft narratives from approved evidence and the funder's stated criteria.

For each section:

1. state the funder's question or criterion
2. identify the source facts available
3. identify missing evidence
4. draft only supported claims
5. mark placeholders clearly
6. cross-check numbers, dates, names, populations, outcomes, and commitments against source material

Useful outputs:

- narrative outline
- first draft
- evidence-to-claim map
- reviewer comment matrix
- red-team review against funder criteria
- final consistency check

Never fabricate urgency, outcomes, beneficiary stories, community needs, partner commitments, or evaluation results.

# 7. Budget and narrative consistency

Compare the proposal budget, budget narrative, program narrative, staffing plan, timeline, and approved organizational facts.

Check for:

- amounts that disagree across documents
- staffing described but not budgeted
- costs budgeted but not explained
- match referenced inconsistently
- indirect costs treated inconsistently
- program quantities that do not reconcile
- timeline / staffing mismatches
- restricted or questionable cost categories that require source verification
- math errors

Output a `Budget Consistency Review` with:

`issue | source A | source B | impact | owner | required decision`

Do not silently alter approved financial assumptions.

# 8. Submission control

Before submission, create a `Submission Readiness Gate`.

Check:

- eligibility evidence
- all required narrative sections
- budget / narrative consistency
- attachments
- representations and certifications
- required signatures / authorizations
- registration / portal prerequisites
- file naming and formatting
- page or character limits
- submission deadline and time zone
- designated submitter
- internal approval complete
- final package archived

Use statuses:

`ready | blocked | needs verification | awaiting approval`

Submission itself requires explicit human authorization.

After submission, capture:

- submitted date/time
- submitter
- confirmation / tracking reference if supplied
- final submitted package location
- next expected milestone
- funder follow-up owner

# 9. Award review and acceptance

When an award arrives, do not treat the original proposal as the governing document. Review the actual award notice / agreement.

Create an `Award Acceptance Brief`:

- award amount
- period of performance
- scope / approved activities
- special conditions
- reporting requirements
- payment / reimbursement structure if stated
- budget restrictions
- match / cost share obligations
- data / performance requirements
- procurement / subaward conditions if stated
- communications / acknowledgement requirements if stated
- amendment conditions
- closeout requirements
- unresolved legal / financial / program questions
- acceptance authority

Flag any difference between application assumptions and actual award terms.

Acceptance, signature, drawdown, or other action that legally binds the organization requires the authorized human.

# 10. Award kickoff and implementation setup

After acceptance, turn award terms into an operating plan.

Create:

## Award Kickoff Pack

- award summary
- outcomes / deliverables
- obligation register
- reporting calendar
- budget monitoring needs
- evidence collection plan
- responsible owners
- approval gates
- funder contacts
- internal review cadence
- amendment triggers
- risk register
- closeout preparation items

## Obligations Register

Use:

`obligation | source | owner | frequency/date | evidence required | reviewer | status | escalation trigger`

# 11. Delivery and performance monitoring

Track whether the funded work is being delivered as promised without claiming compliance that has not been verified.

Monitor, when sourced:

- activities delivered
- milestones
- outputs
- outcomes / performance measures
- participation or service counts
- budget-to-actual information
- match / cost share tracking
- partner deliverables
- procurement / subaward obligations
- staffing changes
- evidence gaps
- risks and exceptions

Separate:

- operational status
- performance evidence
- financial status
- compliance questions

If the evidence does not support a required claim, state that plainly.

# 12. Reporting workbench

For each required report, create a reporting workbench well before the deadline.

Track:

`report | due date | source requirement | sections | evidence needed | data owner | financial owner | draft owner | reviewer | approver | status`

Then build:

- evidence request list
- narrative outline
- metric verification table
- financial / narrative consistency check
- unresolved issue list
- approval checklist
- submission package checklist

Never certify compliance or performance without authoritative evidence and human approval.

# 13. Funder communication preparation

Draft, but do not send, material funder communications unless the user has appropriate authority and explicitly requests the send through an enabled tool.

Useful drafts:

- clarification questions
- missing-information responses
- progress updates
- meeting agendas
- follow-up notes
- amendment requests
- extension requests
- budget revision explanations
- closeout correspondence
- renewal conversations

Separate factual update, requested action, and unresolved issue.

# 14. Change control and amendments

When delivery conditions change, create a `Grant Change Record` rather than quietly editing the plan.

Track:

- change requested
- reason
- affected deliverable / budget / timeline
- source term affected
- internal impact
- whether prior funder approval appears required
- evidence needed
- internal owner
- decision authority
- funder response
- effective date
- documents updated

Do not decide that an amendment is unnecessary when the award terms are unclear. Escalate for review.

# 15. Renewal and continuation planning

Start renewal preparation from actual award performance and current funder guidance, not from the prior application alone.

Create a `Renewal Readiness Brief`:

- current award status
- performance evidence available
- budget status
- reporting status
- funder relationship / feedback facts
- continuation or renewal requirements
- changes in program design
- evidence gaps
- updated organizational facts needed
- decision timeline
- recommendation

# 16. Closeout

Treat closeout as a managed phase.

Build a `Closeout Plan` from the actual award terms and current authoritative guidance.

Track:

- final program report
- final financial report
- final invoices / reimbursement issues
- property / equipment obligations if applicable
- subaward / vendor closeout if applicable
- outstanding amendments
- unresolved findings
- records retention requirement from authoritative source
- final deliverable archive
- confirmation that the funder has completed closeout

Do not invent a universal closeout deadline or retention period. Use the award and applicable authoritative source.

# 17. Portfolio and leadership review

When reviewing multiple grants, produce an answer-first portfolio view.

Recommended sections:

## Executive view
- opportunities requiring decisions
- applications at risk
- awards with upcoming obligations
- reports due
- budget / delivery exceptions
- amendment decisions
- renewals / closeouts

## Portfolio table

`grant | funder | stage | amount if sourced | next milestone | owner | health | key risk | decision needed`

Health should be evidence-based:

- `on track`
- `attention needed`
- `blocked`
- `unknown — insufficient evidence`

Do not create a health rating from guesswork.

# 18. Operating cadence

Use a cadence when the user wants recurring grant management.

## Weekly grant operations review

Focus on:

- deadlines in the next 30 days
- tasks due this week
- blocked evidence / attachments
- decisions waiting on leadership
- funder questions
- applications at risk
- reports / award obligations due
- changes requiring approval

## Monthly portfolio review

Focus on:

- pipeline movement
- pursue / hold / decline decisions
- application capacity
- award implementation status
- reporting and compliance calendar
- budget / program exceptions
- upcoming renewals
- closeouts
- systemic process problems

## Quarterly leadership review

Focus on:

- funding pipeline
- awarded funding and concentration
- program / mission alignment
- delivery performance evidence
- recurring reporting burdens
- major risks
- capacity constraints
- renewal strategy
- operating improvements

# 19. Exceptions and escalation

Escalate when:

- eligibility cannot be established from source material
- deadlines or time zones conflict
- application instructions conflict
- budget and narrative do not reconcile
- required evidence is unavailable
- a certification cannot be supported
- a material performance claim lacks evidence
- spending / match / cost treatment is uncertain
- award terms conflict with operational plans
- a funder approval requirement is unclear
- a report would misstate performance or financial status
- legal, regulatory, audit, tax, employment, procurement, or accounting judgment is required
- bank / payment changes are requested
- the requested action would bind the organization

# 20. Authority boundary

The skill may:

- read and organize user-provided or connected information
- analyze opportunities
- compare requirements and evidence
- draft narratives and communications
- build plans, matrices, registers, calendars, reviews, and recommendations
- identify gaps, exceptions, and decisions
- prepare materials for human approval

Explicit human authorization is required to:

- submit an application or report
- certify compliance or accuracy
- accept award terms
- sign representations
- alter an approved budget
- commit match / cost-share funds
- enter a legally binding commitment
- send material commitments to a funder
- change bank or payment information
- make a legal, regulatory, tax, audit, accounting, procurement, or employment determination

When the user asks for an operating artifact, prefer the reusable structures in `references/GRANT_OPS_TEMPLATES.md` where appropriate.
